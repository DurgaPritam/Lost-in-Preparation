**Solvent descriptor validation** against experimental logP (SangsterLogP (Zenodo 19387552, CC BY 4.0), neutral subset; 150 compounds, xTB succeeded for 150):

**Training-overlap check.** RDKit's Crippen logP follows Wildman & Crippen, J. Chem. Inf. Comput. Sci. 39, 868-873 (1999); its training molecules are not distributed with RDKit, so exact overlap is **not determinable**. Proxy: compounds whose every cited measurement source was first published in 2000 or later (n = 67) vs earlier (n = 43; 40 with an unresolved year):

| Subset | RDKit MAE | xTB MAE |
|---|---|---|
| sources from 2000 on | 0.824 (0.699–0.950) | 1.482 (1.249–1.727) |
| sources before 2000 | 0.530 (0.398–0.672) | 1.928 (1.496–2.436) |

RDKit's error is larger on post-1999 data, which is consistent with (but does not prove) a training-overlap advantage on older data; RDKit still outperforms xTB on the post-1999 subset. xTB logP = log K(wet octanol / water) from ALPB `woctanol` and `water`.

Default descriptor: **rdkit** (lower MAE). Caveat: Crippen's parameters were fitted to public logP data that may overlap this set; xTB is unfitted. 95% CIs are paired bootstrap (B = 2000).

### Why cold water gives artemisinin 0.27 (diagnostic; no model change)

Source: `data/interim/m4/water_calibration.json`; figure: [docs/figures/water_likelihood_vs_logp.svg](docs/figures/water_likelihood_vs_logp.svg).

- A filtered, cold, aqueous preparation has heat = filtration = acidity = 1, so the prediction is the water window alone: `f = sigmoid(k * (upper - logP)); no solubility, melting-point, dose or contact-time term`, with upper ~ U(1.0, 2.5) and k ~ U(1.0, 2.0). For the study-set compounds it is a one-variable function of logP: Spearman ρ = -0.9971 (n = 2530; 31 compounds sit below the curve because of the MW > 1000 size penalty).
- The median crosses 0.40 at logP 2.004, 0.30 at logP 2.297 and 0.10 at logP 3.278. The 'undecided' band 0.30–0.40 is only 0.29 logP units wide, narrower than the descriptor's RMSE against experiment (0.8374), and the median study-set compound has logP 2.439, on the steepest part of the curve.
- Artemisinin's Crippen logP is 2.395, and sigmoid(k_med · (upper_med − logP)) = 0.2754 reproduces the Monte Carlo median of 0.2749. At the experimental logP 2.9 (PubChem CID 68827, LogP (SangsterLogP)) the same model gives 0.158 (0.0129–0.6172). Part of the 0.27 is descriptor error; the rest is the window's position and shallow slope.
- **Low water solubility is not in the model.** It sees only logP, with no solubility, melting-point (crystal-lattice), dose or contact-time term, so it cannot represent 'poorly soluble in absolute terms'. A low amount in a large volume of water may still dissolve completely. PubChem's artemisinin record lists no experimental solubility, so no solubility value is used here.
- **Proposal (not adopted).** Fix the window slope at ln 10 (the slope of a two-phase partition equilibrium) and model descriptor error explicitly. This was pre-registered as the secondary variant and evaluated only on v2 rows (above). A solubility/dose term would need per-compound melting points or solubilities and plant-content ranges that the repo does not have; it is not proposed yet.
- The recorded artemisinin result and every threshold are unchanged. Separately, the v2 rows include a published report of artemisinin present in *Artemisia annua* (hot water infusion (tea), PMC13471599), which the primary model scores 0.16 (disagrees).
