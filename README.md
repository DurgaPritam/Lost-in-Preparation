# Lost in Preparation

> The plant may be right. The preparation may be wrong.

**Lost in Preparation** reads historical herbal recipes, models what each preparation would deliver chemically, and audits whether modern studies tested the remedy *the way it was made*. Its output is a ranked list of **hypotheses worth re-testing**, each prepared as the recipe said. It never claims that a remedy works or is safe.

In the 1970s, Tu Youyou's team kept failing to get antimalarial activity from sweet wormwood until a 4th-century text by Ge Hong pointed them away from heat. A low-temperature extraction then yielded artemisinin (Nobel Prize, 2015). The plant was right all along; the preparation was not.

The headline result is a **reporting gap**. Modern studies of these plants rarely state how the plant was prepared (heat, fresh or dried, filtration), and very few compare two preparations of the same material. That makes it hard to tell whether a remedy was ever tested the way it was made (descriptive findings with n and 95% CIs, below). The audit of fidelity to the historical preparation is supporting detail, with small n. The preparation model behind the hypotheses is a **transparent hypothesis generator**. It is best supported for essential-oil compounds and unvalidated for aqueous preparations, and its ranges are wide once descriptor error is included (see *What the results support* below).

## What the results support

**Headline: the reporting gap.** These are descriptive findings, not hypothesis tests (`data/interim/m5/reporting_gap.json`, generated by `pipeline/lip/m5/reporting_gap.py`). "Stated" means the rule-based extractor coded a value. It is a heuristic; its human hand-check is pending, and an AI-assisted sensitivity check (provisional) follows the list.

- **Studies that prepared the plant material** (audit v2 eligibility, n = 43): share stating each preparation variable:
  solvent 86% (37/43; 95% CI 73%–93%); heat 26% (11/43; 95% CI 15%–40%); plant part 49% (21/43; 95% CI 35%–63%); fresh or dried 16% (7/43; 95% CI 8%–30%); filtration 12% (5/43; 95% CI 5%–24%).
  - All five stated: 0% (0/43; 95% CI 0%–8%). None stated: 5% (2/43; 95% CI 1%–15%).
  - Eligibility partly selects on a stated solvent, so the solvent share in this set is inflated by construction.
- **Every coded study retrieved for a species–indication pair** (audit v1, n = 303): none of the five stated 43% (131/303; 95% CI 38%–49%); all five 1% (4/303; 95% CI 1%–3%). Heat stated: 4% (9/241; 95% CI 2%–7%) when coded from abstracts, 45% (28/62; 95% CI 33%–57%) from open-access methods sections. Filtration stated: 1% (3/241; 95% CI 0%–4%) from abstracts, 44% (27/62; 95% CI 32%–56%) from methods.
- **Direct comparisons are rare.** In the pre-registered v3 screening, 6 of 257 article screenings whose tables were examined (249 distinct articles) compared two preparations of the same plant material in a usable table: 2.3% (95% CI 1.1%–5.0%).

- ***AI-assisted sensitivity check (provisional).*** If the extractor's error rates from the AI-assisted check (`data/interim/m5/reporting_gap_sensitivity.json`) applied to every coded study, the prepared-set shares would be: heat 26% → 24% (95% CI 14%–35%); fresh or dried 16% → 19% (95% CI 8%–30%); plant part 49% → 49% (95% CI 33%–66%); solvent 86% → 79% (95% CI 62%–93%). Filtration was not in the AI check, so it is not adjusted (frozen 12%).
  - On the 40 checked studies themselves, the share stating each variable: heat 12/40 by the extractor vs 12/40 by the AI coder; fresh or dried 11/40 by the extractor vs 11/40 by the AI coder; plant part 16/40 by the extractor vs 20/40 by the AI coder; solvent 20/40 by the extractor vs 22/40 by the AI coder.
  - At study level the extractor's misses are largely offset by values it records that the AI coder left unknown, so the headline shares barely move. The AI coder is not a person, and only 10 of the 40 checked studies are in the prepared set. The frozen numbers above are unchanged.

**Supporting detail: fidelity to the historical preparation** (the audit is frozen; v1 and v2 are reported as they are; small n).
- **v1 (as pre-registered):** 1 of 67 studies with a known status were faithful (277 unknown). Its solvent rule makes every non-water recipe unmatchable, so this mostly reflects the protocol.
- **v2 (pre-registered re-analysis after v1's design flaw):** 0 of 11 known units were faithful (32 unknown, 4 with no modern analogue).
  - Solvent matched in 4 of 12 known units (95% CI 11%–56%); plant part in 4 of 9; heat was known for only 2.
  - These counts are too small to estimate a field-wide fidelity rate. The reporting gap above is the reason.

**Hypotheses worth re-testing.** 3 ranked from the audit (v1; v2 in RESULTS). The top one, *Glycyrrhiza glabra* for liver diseases, changed after a logged extraction correction (median gap 0.20 → 0.42; see the liquorice case study in RESULTS).

**The preparation model is a transparent hypothesis generator, not a validated predictor.** Every factor and assumption is inspectable, and every estimate carries a range. Its support differs by preparation:
- **Essential-oil compounds: best supported.** In the pre-registered v1 check, 18/18 essential-oil rows agree with the model. These rows test presence only; no essential-oil row reported a compound as absent.
- **Aqueous preparations (infusions, decoctions, juices): unvalidated.** The pre-registered v2 check found 2 `absent/low` rows against a quota of 15 (agreement 0/2). The paired v3 check is **inconclusive** (5 articles). Single-row checks also measure detection, not the extraction fraction the model predicts (METHODS §9).
- **Wide ranges.** With descriptor error included, the median width of the 5th–95th range across study-set compounds is 0.45 for cold water infusion, 0.44 for hot water infusion (tea), 0.35 for decoction in water on a 0–1 scale.
- **Ranking.** Hypotheses are ranked only on profile gaps that hold across those ranges (METHODS §11).

## Limitations

The model predicts the **fraction** of a compound that a preparation extracts. Published studies record whether a compound was **detected**, which also depends on how much the plant contains and on the detection limit. LOTUS has no abundance data, so single-row literature checks cannot separate the two. The paired v3 check compares compounds within one plant and study to cancel plant content, but too few studies made such comparisons for it to be conclusive. Pliny-derived results are lower confidence than Culpeper's. Every reference used so far was made by an AI and awaits human review. See [METHODS.md §12](METHODS.md#12-limitations).

## Reproduce

```bash
python3.11 -m venv .venv && .venv/bin/pip install -r requirements.txt   # exact versions (Python 3.11)
export PYTHONPATH=pipeline:.
.venv/bin/python -m lip fetch --verify # raw texts (not committed); checksums pinned in data/raw/manifest.json
.venv/bin/python -m lip ingest         # passages + crosswalk
.venv/bin/python -m lip contract       # contract JSON Schemas
.venv/bin/python -m lip m1 sample      # gold sample + annotation tool data
.venv/bin/python -m pytest
```

**Fresh-clone test, no keys.** `scripts/repro_fresh_clone.sh` clones the repository into a temporary directory and installs `requirements.txt`. Every pipeline step then runs with `LIP_CACHE_ONLY=1`, no API keys and an empty home directory. `LIP_CACHE_ONLY=1` refuses every network connection, so a missing cache fails loudly instead of being downloaded silently. The only network step is `lip fetch --verify`, which downloads the four public-domain texts and checks their pinned checksums. Afterwards the script compares every regenerated file with the commit; a file whose only differences are timestamps counts as reproduced. Tests that need a git-ignored download cache (PubChem, EMA/PMC sources, Wikipedia, tiktoken's encoding) skip when it is missing, naming the cache and the command that fetches it. Results are in [docs/PHASE9_CHECKLIST.md](docs/PHASE9_CHECKLIST.md).

**Extraction.** The results use extractions written in a Claude Code session (`data/m1_session/`), checked for schema and verbatim quotes with `python -m lip m1 session-ingest --scope sample` (or `--scope approved`). This step needs no API key or network. The API extractor (`python -m lip m1 extract`; Groq by default, Ollama and Claude optional in `config/pipeline.toml`) is kept. Its cached Groq responses are committed but excluded from evaluation. For Groq, put `GROQ_API_KEY=...` in your environment or a local `.env` file (see `.env.example`; `.env` is git-ignored).

## Website

`site/` is a Next.js (App Router, TypeScript strict) static export. It reads only `site-data/` at build time:
- **Contract types:** the TypeScript types and Zod schemas are generated from the contract JSON Schemas.
- **Contract check:** every file is checked against the contract schemas, and the build fails on any mismatch.
- **Screens:** the landing page with a live mini-demo, the Preparation Lab, the Recipe Atlas and recipe pages, the evidence gap, the liquorice case study, the Lab Planner (a placeholder until the quantum feature ships), Methods & data, and Credits.
- **Preparation Lab:** computes each profile in the browser with the preparation engine ([site/lib/engine/](site/lib/engine/)), in a Web Worker. The engine is the M4 model ported to TypeScript, and the build fails unless it reproduces every published Python estimate exactly. Every Lab configuration is a shareable URL (`/lab/?plant=&solvent=&heat=&strained=&acid=`).
- **Botanical plates:** 19th-century plates whose Wikimedia Commons licence field says public domain (each still to be confirmed by a person), downloaded once by `python -m lip plates` and served locally; credits are in [docs/IMAGE_CREDITS.md](docs/IMAGE_CREDITS.md) and on `/credits/`.
- **Changes from the design:** listed in [site/CHANGES.md](site/CHANGES.md).

```bash
cd site && npm ci
npm run build        # generate contract types, validate site-data, export to site/out/
npm run serve        # http://localhost:4517
npm run ci           # type-check, lint, Vitest, build (with the engine parity gate), Playwright + axe (both themes, desktop and 390px), Lighthouse in both themes (medians in site/perf/)
```

CI runs the same gates in `.github/workflows/site.yml`. Deployment notes are in [site/DEPLOY.md](site/DEPLOY.md).
