Why preparation matters · motivation, not proof

One plant, two preparations

  1. 1 / 3

    The plant was right

    In the 4th century CE, Ge Hong’s Handbook of Prescriptions for Emergencies listed qinghao (Artemisia annua) for intermittent fevers.

  2. 2 / 3

    The preparation was wrong

    When Tu Youyou’s team screened qinghao in the late 1960s and 1970s, their extracts gave inconsistent results. Their standard hot extraction appeared to destroy the activity.

  3. 3 / 3

    Reading the recipe again

    Ge Hong described soaking the herb in water and wringing out the juice, with no heating. A low-temperature ether extraction recovered the activity and led to the isolation of artemisinin.

This is one celebrated case. It shows that preparation can decide what a recipe delivers. It does not show that other historical recipes hide overlooked preparations. Testing that, recipe by recipe, with its uncertainty shown, is what this tool is for.

Account based on Tu Y. (2011), “The discovery of artemisinin (qinghaosu) and gifts from Chinese medicine”, Nature Medicine 17, 1217–1220.
